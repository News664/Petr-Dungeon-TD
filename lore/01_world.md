# The world

## The catastrophe

**Fixed.** A phenomenon is spreading through the world that turns women to stone. The change is called solidification. It is gradual until it is total: a person can carry some of it and recover, but once it is complete she is a statue. A statue can still be restored if she is brought to a protected place within thirty days. After thirty days abandoned, nothing brings her back.

**Fixed.** The world had many civilizations, all of women, and from time to time champions arrived from other worlds. Nearly all of them are stone now. Monsters are largely untouched by the phenomenon and have taken what was left.

**Fixed.** Nobody knows where it comes from. Finding out, and stopping it, is the purpose of the whole campaign.

**Proposed.** The survivors call it **the Stilling**. It was not sudden. It came over years, from the deep places outward: first the miners and delvers, then the low towns, then everyone. People had time to understand what was happening to them and no way to stop it. The roads are lined with those who were walking toward somewhere they thought was safe.

**Proposed.** The Stilling is strongest underground and near anything that came from underground. It is carried on a pale dust and on the gaze or breath of certain creatures, but it is also simply present in the air of the fallen world, slow and patient. This is why ordinary people cannot leave warded ground, and why workers in a secured stratum need wards every day.

**Open.** Why women only, what the source is, and whether it is a thing, a place or a will.

## The sanctuary

**Fixed.** One protected place remains, kept by a goddess whose strength is almost gone. A small remnant of the original people lives there: artisans, engineers, workers. They are not fighters.

**Proposed.** The sanctuary was a hill shrine and its village, never a great city. It survived because it was small and because she was there. It has a wall, a gate, a guild hall built around the old summoning seal, a recovery ward, workshops, and gardens that still grow. A few dozen people live in it. Everyone knows everyone.

**Proposed.** Inside the walls the air is clean. This is the goddess's doing and it costs her. Everything the guild does with ward energy is an attempt to take that burden from her and carry it with crystal and craft instead.

## The goddess

**Fixed.** She is weak. She spent what she had left to wake the seal for the last summoner, and to sponsor the first champions. She can still do one great thing at a time: when the frontier first falls she can banish the attackers and fully restore about half of the fallen defenders. Doing so empties her, and the wards over the secured ground falter until the frontier is retaken. She recovers that one great act only after the guild has held two full attacks with its territory intact.

**Fixed.** Her small help is different from her miracle. She can guide rescuers to statues at a rebuilt frontier. She cannot revive them.

**Proposed.** She was a goddess of thresholds and safe return: doors, hearths, the lamp left in the window. It is why the seal answers to her and why she can hold a wall but cannot march. Being this weak has made her plain-spoken. She does not command the player; she asks, and she says thank you.

**Open.** Her name. Whether other gods existed and what became of them.

## The seal and the champions

**Fixed.** The player is the last living summoner, and guild master. An old seal in the guild hall can call women from other worlds. Summoning takes scarce resources and the seal needs time between uses. Each champion is an individual who persists, grows, and can be lost.

**Fixed.** In an emergency the seal can be forced. A champion called this way arrives at once but does not hold: she fades back to her own world after about two weeks.

**Proposed.** The seal does not seize people. It asks, across worlds, and a woman who answers has chosen to. This matters: every champion is here because she said yes to a stranger's need. Most were at an ending of their own when the call came: a war just lost, a post just abandoned, a long illness, a locked door. None of them can go home while the seal is weak, and they know it.

**Proposed.** Champions are not immune. They are from elsewhere, so the Stilling takes them more slowly than it took the people of this world, slowly enough to fight in. That is the whole of their advantage.

**Open.** Whether champions can ever return home, and whether they would choose to.

## The labyrinth

**Fixed.** Beneath and beyond the sanctuary lies a labyrinth of many layers, called strata, each a handful of floors deep and each ruled by a powerful creature. Killing that creature scatters the organised monsters of the stratum. Workers can then extract resources there under wards. The answer to the catastrophe is somewhere at the bottom.

**Fixed.** On a regular rhythm, about every seven days, something stirs the monsters and they attack the guild's forward line. The attacks can be foreseen. The guild's frontier is always at the edge of the deepest stratum it has secured.

**Proposed.** The labyrinth was not built as one thing. It is the buried past of this world, layer under layer: each stratum is a place people once lived, sunk and sealed by the Stilling when it rose through it. Going down is going back in time, toward wherever it began.

**Proposed.** The weekly stirring is called the **surge**. It rises from below like a tide, and the monsters move ahead of it. The residents can feel it coming a day or two before: bells hum, ward-crystals dim.

## Monsters

**Fixed.** Monsters resist solidification. Some of them cause it.

**Proposed.** They are not invaders. They are what lives in stone and dust and always did, deep down, and the Stilling has made the whole world into their kind of place. They do not hate. The ones that solidify are not casting a curse; it is how they feed, or see, or breathe.

**Proposed.** The masters of the strata are different. Each was something else once: a guardian, a monument, a ruler. What they are now is the Stilling wearing that shape.
