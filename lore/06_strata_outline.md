# Strata outline

**Everything here is Proposed unless marked.** It answers question 11 with a suggestion to react to. The first two strata exist in the game; the second is a first version.

**Fixed.** More than one medium is possible, but not in the early strata; the first stratum is stone. One person is turned into one medium only, never a mixture. The game's rules already carry this: whatever first touches a champion decides what she is turning into until she is clear of it.

## The idea

Iwanaga wants variety. Her labyrinth is a museum with wings, each kept by a curator who works in her own medium. Going down, the party passes through the collection in the order she values it: the commonplace near the top, the prized at the bottom.

The rules do not change from wing to wing. There is one gauge and one thirty-day limit everywhere. What changes is what a statue is made of, how the place looks, and what the curator does with the people she keeps.

## Five wings

| # | Name | Medium | What it was | Its curator | Speaks? | What the party learns |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Pale Galleries | Stone | Halls of art in the hill country | The Hollow Colossus: an unfinished monument the Stilling filled | No | She uses the stilled as furniture. |
| 2 | The Drowned Cloister | Stone still, or the first new medium (ice) | A lake monastery, sunk and frozen from the bottom up | The Abbess of Still Water: robed, courteous, keeps her sisters in the ice of the cloister walls | Yes | The stilled are sorted and valued. Someone is choosing. |
| 3 | The Gilded Exchange | Metal: bronze and gold | A trading city's great market | The Assayer: a broker who weighs every champion she meets and names a price | Yes | It is a collection, and the seal is why this world was chosen. |
| 4 | The Glass Conservatory | Glass and crystal | A royal winter garden and its hall of mirrors | The Curator of Likenesses: shows champions what they left behind, and turns that against them | Yes; she alters minds | Iwanaga believes she is saving them. Some of what the Curator says is true. |
| 5 | The Vault | All of them | Not a place that was ever anything else | Iwanaga | Yes | What it costs to end her, and that it will not bring back the lost. |

The fourth curator is the mind-altering master asked for in answer 12.

## What each wing adds to play

| # | New in the labyrinth | New on the walls |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mist, a one-way gate, a roaming elite; then traps and statues used as mechanisms | Runners, brutes, petrifiers |
| 2 | Water that rises and falls and changes which corridors exist; cold that slows the whole party | Attackers that freeze a structure so its occupant cannot act |
| 3 | Locked doors with prices; a creature that steals from the pack | Attackers that carry off a fallen defender's statue unless stopped |
| 4 | Mirrors that show false corridors; a "doubt" status that turns a champion's skill on her allies | Decoys: attackers that are not there |
| 5 | Everything, briefly | A last defense of the sanctuary while the party is below |

## The cost of more than one medium

Each medium needs its own late-stage and statue pictures for every character who can fall there. With four class designs that is manageable: three extra pictures per class per medium. With twenty-four named characters it is a great deal of art.

Three ways to hold it down:

1. **Stone everywhere.** One medium for the whole game. Cheapest; loses the collector's variety in the pictures, though the places can still differ.
2. **Medium by wing, pictures by class.** Named characters get full stage sets in stone only, and use their class's pictures for the other media.
3. **Medium by wing, statue only.** The creeping stages stay as drawn; only the final statue picture changes with the medium.

The game's wording is already neutral, so any of these can be chosen later. Art made for stone now is not wasted under any of them.

## The Drowned Cloister as built

**In the game now.** A lake monastery, sunk. Its water rises and falls twice a day, and the low corridors can only be walked when it is out. Lower down the water has frozen: the deeper the party goes, the colder it gets.

| Floor | Name | What is there |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Flooded Narthex | The entrance halls. A flooded passage is the short way to a store worth working; the long way round is always dry. |
| 2 | The Cloister Walk | The ring the Bellringer walks. The old refectory at its centre, where things still grow, lies behind the water. A pressure plate on the short way down, and the first ice: a frozen stretch of the lower walk. |
| 3 | The Frozen Chapel | The Abbess's chapel. The water guards the short way to her. The dry way crosses a frozen nave where nobody can stop at will: the party slides until a pillar or a wall stops it, and has to find the stopping places that lead to her door. |

Its creatures: Drowned Novices, Cloister Eels, Bell Wardens that toll before they strike, Weeping Icons whose gaze solidifies, and Silt Choirs whose dirge reaches everyone.

**The Abbess of Still Water** speaks. She is courteous and certain, calls Iwanaga "my lady" and Sakuya "my lady's little sister", and treats the party as acquisitions she would like to keep unspoiled. Her lines are in `config/balance.json`.

**Proposed:** the cold and the ice are the first sign of the second medium. Statues here are still stone, but a deeper part of the Cloister, or the next stratum, could be where ice begins to take people. **Not built:** cold that slows the party, attackers that freeze a structure on the frontier, statues kept in the cloister walls, and what the party learns here (that the stilled are sorted and valued: someone is choosing).

## Next: the Gilded Exchange

**Proposed.** The third stratum. In the game it exists by name only: the frontier moves to it once the Abbess falls, and its attackers (husks and bulwarks among them) already come up against the line.

A trading city's great market, sunk whole. Its curator is **the Assayer**, a broker who weighs every champion she meets and names a price for her, out loud. Sakuya's last words in the Cloister warn of it: "everything has a price."

What it could add:

- **Prices instead of locks.** Doors that open for a payment: resources, a carried statue, or a champion's time spent standing in a booth. Every shortcut is a trade.
- **A thief.** A creature that takes one supply from the pack when it touches the party and runs; catch it before the next floor and get it back.
- **Offers.** The Assayer speaks before each fight with an offer: leave one champion with her and the rest may pass. Accepting is always possible and always wrong; refusing makes the fight harder.
- **What it teaches.** The seal is why this world was chosen. The Exchange keeps ledgers of every champion ever summoned, across every world, priced. The guild master's own name is on the newest page.
- **Medium.** Metal is proposed for the Exchange: gilt and bronze. This would be the first stratum where a champion who falls is not stone.
